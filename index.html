⚡ The Problem

Why do AirPods sound
bad on Mac calls?

This is not a bug. It's a fundamental Bluetooth limitation that affects every wireless headset on the market.

📡

Bluetooth has limited bandwidth

Bluetooth is a wireless protocol with a fixed data capacity. When your headphones connect to your Mac, that bandwidth has to be shared between everything happening over the connection.

🔀

Two-way audio kills quality

When your headphones act as both speaker and microphone at the same time, Bluetooth switches to a lower-quality codec (HFP/HSP) to handle both streams, causing that infamous robotic, muffled sound.

📞

Triggered by any call or app

The moment any app tries to access your mic (Zoom, Teams, Discord, FaceTime, even Safari), macOS switches your headphones into this degraded mode. It happens silently, every single time.

🔊

Affects every Bluetooth device

AirPods, AirPods Pro, AirPods Max, Beats, Sony WH/WF series, Bose QuietComfort: if it has Bluetooth and a microphone, it's affected. No firmware update will ever fix this. It's a hardware-level limitation.

🔬 Under the hood

The bandwidth problem, visualised

Bluetooth gives your headphones a fixed "pipe" of bandwidth. Here's what happens when that pipe is full.

❌  Bluetooth headphones used as MIC + SPEAKER
🎵 High-quality audio output (72%)
🎤 Mic (28%, degraded)
⚠️  Result: Bluetooth is forced into low-quality HFP mode. Your voice sounds robotic and muffled.
✅  Bluetooth headphones used as SPEAKER ONLY, Mac mic for input
🎵 Full bandwidth for high-quality audio output (100%)
✅  Result: Full A2DP stereo quality through your headphones. Mac's built-in mic delivers crystal-clear voice.

💡 The Solution

Two ways to fix it

The fix is always the same: keep your Mac's built-in microphone as the input while your headphones handle output. You can do this manually every time, or use an app that does it for you.

🖱 Option 1: Manual

Do it yourself, every time

You can manually fix this each time you connect your Bluetooth headphones by changing the input device in macOS System Settings. It takes about 15 seconds every time and it's easy to forget before important calls.

  1. 1 Connect your Bluetooth headphones as usual
  2. 2 Open System Settings → Sound
  3. 3 Click the Input tab
  4. 4 Select MacBook Microphone (or your preferred built-in mic)
  5. 5 Repeat every single time you reconnect your headphones
  • ✗  Must repeat on every reconnect
  • ✗  Easy to forget before important calls
  • ✗  macOS often reverts automatically
  • ✗  No notification when it changes
